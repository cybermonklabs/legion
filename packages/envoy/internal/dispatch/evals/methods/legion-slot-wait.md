**How it is measured.** The eval counts the project's issues that finished in the window, as legion-work-time counts them. The slot wait of an issue is the hours of its observed life in which its phase is `admitted` and it holds no slot. It counts the whole life, from the creation of the issue to its end, and leaves out its parked hours. The phase is the one the daemon observed. Its `queued` stretches are the admitted hours in which the issue held no slot of its own. An issue that the daemon never observed has no slot wait.

`legionfacts` stores the slot wait of each issue, by the rules of `legion/metrics`. For each UTC day, `metrics.SlotWaitDay` takes the slot wait of each issue that closed that day. `metrics.FoldSlotWait` merges the hours of a window's days. It answers their sum, count, median and 90th percentile. The series has its roots cut (`hours:roots`) and its children cut (`hours:children`).

The day rows start on the first UTC midnight at or after the project's first slot observation. So a window that starts before that observation reads "no data". No other eval holds an hour of slot wait.

**Why this rule is not the rule of flow analysis version 1.** Flow analysis version 1 (dispatch://INFRA-979/artifact/flow-analysis-md) measured slot wait by a status rule, at 39.3 percent of 14,458 hours. This eval uses another rule: the hours of the issue's observed life in which its phase is `admitted` and it holds no slot, with parked hours left out. The status rule counted the hours from an issue's first `todo` to its first worker event. Those hours include time in which the issue held a slot before any worker started. The status rule also counted no wait that came after a worker began. It had to work that way, because Dispatch's statuses cannot see whether an issue holds a slot. This eval reads the daemon's own slot observations, so it counts only the hours in which the issue had no slot. The two rules give different numbers for the same issues. Version 2 of the analysis names this rule beside its value.

**What it does not count.** Parked hours, hours in which the issue held a slot, open issues, and hours before the daemon first observed the issue.
