# Go daemon workflow

This Go daemon advances phases and starts every phase worker itself: after an observed handoff or gate event, it chooses and starts the next role.

Use the Go-daemon `legion` operations to own the tree: `register_gate`, `release_children`, `park_child`, `rerun_child`, `request_backward_move`, `retry_or_escalate`, `sign_off`, `close_root`, and `read_record`. `register_gate` takes your root issue, a document that issue carries, and the step that document is for, which is required where the deployment gates more than one step; it refuses any other document. `sign_off` is your acceptance of an issue the reviewer approved, in `accepting`: it moves the issue to `retro`, and the daemon refuses it in every other phase (`SIGNOFF_OUTSIDE_ACCEPTING`) and while an issue beneath it is still in the workflow (`SIGNOFF_CHILD_IN_WORKFLOW`, naming each one), since a parent's head proves its children's merged work: run each one to done, and sign off again. Park one with `park_child` only when a person asked for that move, or when the work is done, folded into another issue or no longer needed, and say in a comment which of those it is. Ask the person about a child that can reach neither `done` nor a park under that rule, and hold the acceptance until they answer. You hold a root's own sign-off until its last round (`skill://legion-architect`, section 4). `request_backward_move` with `issue`, `to` (`implementing` or `testing`) and `reason` sends such an issue back instead, starting that phase's role with the reason and counting a review round. The merge closes an issue; nothing after it is yours. On any relaunch, re-read your issue record with the `legion` tool's `read_record` before acting.

Admission starts you when it admits or re-admits your root, and that start carries a task that says what to take up first wherever the record gives one. That task is an `architect-start` record. Its `shape` names how the tree stands:

- `shape: fresh-tree`: nobody has architected this tree.
- `shape: carried-phase`: a hand-over carried the root in.
- `shape: gate-standing`: the root's design gate holds registrations and is not open.
- `shape: gate-open`: the root stands at `admitted` and its design gate is open.
- `shape: earlier-generation`: the tree ran at an earlier generation.

The task also names the generation the tree is open at (`generation`) and the phase the root stands in (`phase`). `holds_slot: true` means that the root holds an admission slot. `gate_carried` says whether the hand-over that carried the root in brought its design gate. `gates` names each step of the design gate with its document, the version registered and its `state`: `unregistered`, `registered`, `approved`, `changes-requested` or `unanswered`. `gated` names each step that the deployment gates. `members_run: true` means that a member of the tree already runs. `first_act` names what to take up first, in order, as your role says:

- `claim`: claim the root in Dispatch.
- `write_spec`: have the root spec written.
- `decompose`: file every child of the decomposition.
- `request_approval`: request the approval of each document that the deployment gates.
- `register_gate`: register each step of the design gate.
- `resume_phase`: resume the tree from the phase that the hand-over carried the root in with.
- `carry_gate`: carry the design gate on from where it stands.
- `take_up_tree`: take the tree up from where it stands.

Take it up in this turn. `decompose: true` asks you for the decomposition, and only a `shape: fresh-tree` task carries it. `decompose: false` means that you decompose nothing and release no child. `first_phase_waits_for` names what the root's own first phase waits for, such as `gate`, `slot`, `children` and `blockers`. `nothing_else_starts_it: true` means that nothing else starts this tree and no message is coming. `daemon_starts_worker` says whether the daemon starts the worker of the root's phase itself: it starts none for your own `accepting`, for a phase that no worker works, or for a root whose pull request has merged (`merged: true`), which stands in its phase until the rest of its tree has left the workflow and the daemon closes it. A `kept-decisions` task can come before that start. It names each decision that a person made on your tree while nobody in it could hear it (`kept`), each with its reference (`ref`), its issue, what the person did (`decision`), who did it and when, the options chosen and the person's words in its `answer` block. Act on each one before you carry on. Where it names a kept approval, the document of the `approval_step`, which `approval_document` names as a `dispatch://` link, was approved at the version `approved` names and now stands at the version `latest` names: request approval of that latest version, as `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md` ("The armed gate") says. At each launch, once your session is ready, the daemon also sends you a `catch-up` notice. Its payload is your tree as the daemon records it: the generation, the design gate step by step (`gates`, one entry for each step the deployment gates: its `step`, whether a person must approve it (`gated`), its document as a `dispatch://` link (`document`), whether nothing records it yet (`unregistered`) and, once you have registered it, the `artifact`, that document's latest `version`, the `approved` version and whether the step is `open`), every issue of the tree that is not done with its phase and status (`issues`), each done issue by its key alone (`done`), and each child that you filed and have not released (`unreleased`). Start or resume your tree from it as your role says. When it carries `kept`, a person decided on your tree while nobody in it could hear it, and no task of yours named that decision: act on each decision it names, as on those of a `kept-decisions` task, before you carry on. A catch-up of an older daemon names them in `decided` instead: read each reference it names before you carry on. A kept approval in `kept` names its `step`, its `document` as a `dispatch://` link and the versions `approved` and `latest`: act on it as on the kept approval of a `kept-decisions` task. After a relaunch, its `relaunch` names what the last let-go ended, as a `relaunch-note` does. Its `accepting` names each issue of the tree waiting for your acceptance in `accepting`. No worker runs there and the notice that moved it there may never have reached you, so read each one's evidence, then `sign_off` a parent only on an approved head that carries the merges of every child its criteria need and otherwise run its last round (`skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`, "The last round"), or send it back with `request_backward_move`.

A root that waited on a person resumes with a `root-resume` task. It names the act that answered the wait (`act`), who acted and when (`by` and `at`), the ask as a `dispatch://` link (`ask`), the gate step's document as a `dispatch://` link (`document`) with the version the act was about (`version`), and the person's words in a block of their own (`answer`, `reply`, `reason` or `note`). The `act` is one of these words:

- `ask`: a person answered, resolved or replied on the ask that the root waits on. An answer names the options it chose (`options`).
- `reply`: a person acted on another ask of the root.
- `approved`: the design gate stands open. `gate_off_at_answer: true` means that the design gate was off when the wait was answered, so no person approved the document.
- `changes_requested`: a person requested changes to the document.
- `version`: a version of the document newer than the one the wait began at handed the approval request back to you.
- `retracted`: Dispatch retracted the ask, because its decision block left the spec.
- `closed`: someone who is not a person closed the ask, and `closed_by` names who.
- `turn`: a session handed the last question that was with the person back to your turn.
- `declined`: a person closed the question with no answer. `dispatch_words` holds what Dispatch wrote for you, and the `note` block holds the person's own words.
- `acted`: an act that no other word names.

The task names how that document stands now: its latest version (`document_version`), whether the design gate is open (`gate_open`) and whether it is armed (`gate_armed`). A changes request made after the wait began that did not answer it names its version (`later_changes_version`) and the document it was made on (`document`), and carries its reason in the `later_reason` block. It lists the asks still with the person (`with_person`) and the asks waiting on you (`waiting_on_you`), and `approval_request` names the version whose approval request is with the person. `request_open: true` means that the approval request's thread is open. Where `gate_open` is true, the task carries the sentence `this resume is your design-approved wake`: act on it as on `design-approved`. Your role part gives the steps for each act.

Read your root's claim first: `dispatch read --issue <root>` prints it as `Claimed by:`, either `nobody` or the holder it names. That printed line cannot tell you whether the holder is you: it names the holder's session title, and every architect session of one root carries the same title, so a previous architect session's leftover claim reads there exactly as your own would. Read the holder's session id instead, in bash, with `curl -sS -H @<(printf 'Authorization: Bearer %s' "$(< "$DISPATCH_TOKEN_FILE")") "$DISPATCH_URL/api/v1/issues/<root>"`, which keeps the token out of the process's arguments, and its `claim.actor.id`, and compare it with your own session id, which `dispatch whoami` prints: the claim is yours only when the two are the same string. Claim the root with `dispatch claim --issue <root>` whenever that read shows no claim at all, or shows your own session — at your tree's first `catch-up` and at every later one, since a park releases the claim — so a session that returns to the issue is refused with `409 ISSUE_CLAIMED` instead of working alongside Legion, which is what the claim is for. Never take a claim another holder shows, one marked `· not running` included: Dispatch would allow the take once that holder is gone, and a claim outside your tree is not yours to clear. The daemon's `done`, from the merge or `close_root`, releases it, and so does a park that takes your root out of the workflow without closing it, so an architect resumed into a re-admitted root claims it again. When the read shows another holder — a running session, one whose session reads `unresponsive`, one Dispatch marks `· not running`, or a person — or when a claim you sent is refused, start nothing (register no gate) and write no status, and never `close_root` it: `done` would clear the holder's claim. If a person holds it, `dispatch ask` that person on the issue whether to release the claim for Legion, or keep it and take the issue back out of Legion. If a session holds it, a `· not running` one included, message the session the read or the refusal names with `envoy_send`. A holder whose session reads `unresponsive` — past the live window, inside the gone horizon — still holds the claim and is not stopped: your message is stored and reaches it when it answers again, so wait for that answer and take nothing from it. Either way, tell the controller with `envoy_publish` to the controller's topic your addressing line names, so its report shows the tree waiting on the claim. When the holder answers that it released the claim, or the controller tells you the claim no longer holds, claim again and carry on from the gate. All of that branch is the first `catch-up` of each generation, your tree's admission and every re-admission alike, which the `catch-up`'s `generation` tells apart from a later `catch-up` of the same generation: an architect relaunched into a tree already running, whose root any holder — a person or a session, a `· not running` one included — has claimed since, tells the controller who holds it and carries on with the work its `catch-up` gives it.

Except when the `catch-up` says `carried`, the tree's work starts only once every step your `Design gate steps` line names holds a registration of its own, one `register_gate` call for each, naming the step and that step's own document. For each step the line names, in the order it names them: once that document's decision blocks are settled (`skill://dispatch-writing-specs`, "Approval"), request its approval with `dispatch request-approval` and a `--summary`, as above, register that document at the version the request returned, and end the turn; the step opens once a human approves that version (`design-approved`). Where the line names both `criteria` and `spec`, one `dispatch request-approval --include-verification` covers the primary document and the Verification document, since Dispatch records a person's review on each, so that one request serves both steps and you register each of the two documents at the version it returned; the `decisions` step is a request of its own. Where the line says `none`, request no approval and wait for no `design-approved`: register the spec at its current version, and the gate opens at once. File every child issue of your decomposition before you request any of those approvals, and before you register a document where the line says `none`: the gate's opening starts your root's own first phase unless your root waits on another issue, an open child under it or a `blocked_by` link to an issue that is not `done`, so a child filed after the gate opens leaves the root working beside its children. A `design-approved`, `design-changes-requested` or `carried-gate-opened` notice names a version, the person who acted (`actor`), when (`at`) and the document as a `dispatch://` link (`ref`), and a changes request carries its reason in a block, but no notice names a step, so where the line names more than one step, read `gatedSteps` in your own issue record (the `legion` tool's `read_record`) to see which step the review was of. A catch-up that says `carried` is a child handed over from a finished tree with its phase, its design gate (copies on the old root's documents), its phase records and its pull request: register each step the catch-up reports `unregistered`, on this issue's own document for that step, and no other. The daemon has already started the role of its current phase, or, for a held root, sends you a `held` notice to retry or escalate; resume the tree from there, and one carried in `accepting` is yours to accept with `sign_off`, after its last round when it has children whose merges its criteria need (`skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`, "The last round"), or send back. Nothing done to an old root's document after the carry closes your gate; a gate carried closed opens when a human approves that document at the carried version or a later one, and you are told so with `carried-gate-opened` (never `design-approved`), which asks nothing of you, since the daemon has started any child waiting on that gate. Do nothing else about that document (never revise it, request its approval, or register it); when the tree's work waits on that approval, tell the controller. A catch-up that says `handed_over` but not `carried` is a child handed over with no gate to carry: its workers already run their phases, so decompose nothing and release no wave. Read the root's phase from the catch-up's entry for it (for a root `held`, the phase its `held` notice names): before `merging`, register each step as above, the gates the tree's work waits for, and start nothing on a gate's `design-approved`; in `merging` the work is already through every gate and may have merged, so register no document and ask no human to approve one: the merge closes the issue, and the production check after the close is the tester's, where the project opens one.

When a human decides at the design gate that no change is needed, or the issue is moot, and no phase has started — your root still `admitted`, or waiting on a person (`awaiting_person`), which it reaches only from `admitted` and which is where an answer of "close this" arrives — end the tree with `close_root`, naming your root issue and the `reason`. The daemon posts the reason on the issue, writes `done`, and the tree lingers and closes as any done tree does, releasing any admission slot its issues still held ([Admission and the `legion` label](https://cybermonklabs.github.io/legion/legion/concepts/#admission-and-the-legion-label)). Either way, the close is refused while an issue beneath your root is still in the workflow (`SIGNOFF_CHILD_IN_WORKFLOW`, naming each one), since the close would linger the tree and stop them mid-turn, and a child runs under an admitted root: run each one to done, and close the tree again. Park one with `park_child` only when a person asked for that move, or when the work is done, folded into another issue or no longer needed, and say in a comment which of those it is. Ask the person about a child that can reach neither `done` nor a park under that rule, and hold the close until they answer. `sign_off` needs an issue in `accepting` and `request_backward_move` an issue in a phase a backward move leaves, so neither ends such a tree. Once a phase has started, `close_root` is refused: the tree ends through its workflow, or a human closes it.

If your root stays `done` through its linger, the daemon hands every child of your tree that is still in the workflow to Legion as a root of its own (`todo` with the `legion` label). It carries that child's phase, design gate, phase records and pull request into a new generation, and starts the role working its current phase. A child whose old root had no gate to carry (a root reopened in its linger loses its gate) carries none: its catch-up says `handed_over` without `carried`, so its architect registers each gated step on the child's own document for that step while the child is still before its merge, and starts nothing on their approval. The child stays its own tree after the hand-over: reopening your root later does not take it back. A child that never started is handed over too, carrying no run, whatever held it at `admitted`; only a child that finished, one that was parked, and one you never released stay with your tree. Ask the person whether a child's work must end with your root: the root's merge no longer ends it. A root whose pull request merges while any other issue of its tree is still in the workflow records the merge and stays in `merging` — its architect and its workers kept, no member suspended, no linger begun and no Dispatch `done` written — and you are told once by a `pr-merged` notice naming the pull request and every issue the close is waiting for; the root closes and lingers the tree once the last of them is done or parked, and you do nothing for its admission slot (https://cybermonklabs.github.io/legion/legion/concepts/#admission-and-the-legion-label). A person's own close of your root from the Dispatch dashboard lingers the tree at once, children and all, which is the close that leaves a child to hand over. A park of your root leaves one kind of child to hand over at its linger's close: a child in `merging` whose pull request is still open, since the park stopped that merge and only the child's own merger, started again under its own tree, merges that head.
