# Go daemon workflow

This Go daemon advances phases and starts every phase worker itself. Do not choose or start the next phase. When testing is complete, end this phase with the `legion` tool: `op: "handoff_complete"`, a `summary`, and `verdict: "pass"` or `verdict: "fail"`; the verdict lets the daemon choose the next transition.

Where this project opens a production check — `production_check` in its `legion.yaml`, which names the tester unless it is `off` — that check is your work after the first fresh read in `verifying`. That read follows the merge and hands this session a task that names no phase and whose first line is `Production check of <KEY>: <title>.`, naming the repository, the pull request, the merge commit, the look's number, what woke it (`the merge` for the first look; `the wake named at the last look — <reason>` for a look your own `wake_at` started, the reason being the summary you sent, or for the retry of a look nobody reported, the reason being that retry's sentence; `the bound: <N days> since the merge` for the bound's look, the check's own bound counted from its opening) and whether it is the last. A session resumed on it tells it from your last phase task by that line. Do what the deployment instructions say to reach production, read whether production carries the merge, then report with `handoff_complete` and a `verdict`, committing nothing and writing no handoff: `pass` once you drove the changed path in production and recorded the result against the issue's `after_merge` check with `dispatch result` at the merge commit, naming what woke this look, with the line it prints in the pull request body; `pending` when production does not carry the merge yet, or carries it with a requirement of the issue that only the deploy can show still unobserved, the summary naming what is missing and what you will look for next, nothing recorded and that line left at `- Check <block id> (after_merge): not yet run`; `fail` for a defect, recorded as a failing result, never an issue you file. A `fail` starts the next change of this issue, so send it to no controller. A defect outside this issue still goes as `skill://legion-worker/references/findings.md` says.

Every look's report names `check_opened_at`, the RFC 3339 time that its task gives, with the stored microseconds unchanged. Only that field selects a check report, on any phase; without it the call completes a step. If a look's task from an earlier release gives no time, read `check.openedAt` with `read_record` and use that value. `pending` and `wake_at` belong only to a report that names its check.

An issue whose Verification document holds no `after_merge` check gives the look no check to record against. Record no result and write no check. An issue in `verifying` is still open, but its checks are the architect's to write, and a closed issue refuses every new document. Name the gap in your pull-request comment and in your report's `summary`, as a named gap, never a failure: the gap alone never makes the verdict `fail`. The verdict follows the rest of the look: `pass` once production carries the merge, and you drove the changed path and each requirement only the deploy can show, and saw each met. `pending` and `fail` follow the rules of any other look.

A `pending` report on a look before the last also names `wake_at`, an RFC 3339 time: when you want your next look. Besides it, only the bound's last look and the retry of a look nobody reported start one (below). A time less than a minute ahead is moved out to a minute, and a time at or after the check's own bound starts no look of its own, since the bound's last look already stands. At that report the daemon ends your process and keeps your session. It keeps the issue's pod while the issue waits in `verifying`, and releases it once the issue is closed. The next look brings back a released pod and resumes this session with a task naming the summary you sent as what woke it. So wait for nothing in this session: end the turn with the report.

Each look is bounded at 2 hours, and the check at the project's `production_check_hours` from its opening. A look nobody reports — its 2 hours, a hold the operator or the controller put on your agent, or a start that failed — is tried again later, and on the last look it closes the check as not run. The bound's task says it is the last: a requirement still unobserved there is a `pending`, which closes the check as not run. That look's `pending` carries no `wake_at`. First write on the pull request why the check did not run within the bound. Before any fact, the daemon refuses a named check that is missing, closed or opened at another time (`CHECK_NOT_OPEN`), then another role (`CHECK_ROLE_MISMATCH`), then a look the claim has not served (`CHECK_LOOK_NOT_SERVED`). Name the check with `check_opened_at` and report from the look's own task. A done issue's call without that field gets `CHECK_LOOK_NOT_SERVED` and records nothing. The daemon also refuses a look already ended (`CHECK_LOOK_NOT_OPEN`), a missing pass, pending or fail verdict (`CHECK_VERDICT_REQUIRED`), a pending before the last look without `wake_at` (`CHECK_WAKE_REQUIRED`, recording nothing and leaving the look open), and a wake on a pass, fail or the last look (`CHECK_WAKE_UNEXPECTED`). The record lives on the pull request and in that result. An issue in `verifying` is open, and a closed issue still takes a result for an `after_merge` or `default_branch` check, a comment and a message. `skill://legion-worker/references/merge-gate.md`, "The production check", says how to reach the deploy and how a `fail` is carried.

A verdict that must carry the daemon's own numbers — what a deployment's idle release has released, resumed and held — reads them with the `legion` tool's `read_release_records`, whose one argument `since` is the RFC 3339 instant your window starts at. What the answer carries is in the tool's own description. It reads them with this session's own claim, so no credential is yours to handle and none is on disk.
