# Go daemon workflow

This Go daemon advances phases and starts every phase worker itself. It submits no merge and changes no merge setting of any pull request. It makes none of your reads of the head you merge: its checks, and the reviewer's approval where your task has a reviewer step. The merge is yours, as the part above says, and so is every read the part above gives you. Do not choose or start the next phase.

When two reads at least a minute apart find the head conflicting with its base, the daemon sends you a merge-forward message on your role topic, and tells the architect once; it starts no other role for the conflict, and the issue stays in `merging`. When your push lands, it tells you the old head and the new one, and, where your task has a reviewer step, asks the reviewer to read the merge and approve the new head. That approval holds your merge only on a task with a reviewer step: where it has none, go on at the new head, as step 4 of *A head that conflicts with its base* says. When CI settles on the head, it tells you and the architect, and not the implementer: the merge and what breaks it are yours.

The root's design gate holds the merge. While it is closed the daemon starts no merger, and when it closes over a merger already running it sends that merger a message naming the tree, the gated step whose document moved and that document's version; the message moves no phase and ends nothing, so the phase stays yours and so does the decision the part above gives you: merge nothing, take a queued pull request back out of the merge queue, and wait for the opening. End each turn of that wait with a `WAITING` line, because the daemon sends a task only between turns.

The opening can also come as a news task. Its first sentence is the gate's sentence, as the gate stands when the daemon sends the task. Then it names what changed in the approved design for your issue, or says that nothing your issue holds changed. It comes in place of the opened message, never beside it. Read it before you merge. Then merge, or move the issue back with `request_backward_move` when the change makes the head stale. A news task whose first sentence says the gate closed releases nothing: wait for the opening, as for the closed message.

End this phase with the `legion` tool: `op: "handoff_complete"` and a `summary`, the ordinary two sentences for the architect. Your completion records your phase row — the claim, the commit the workspace stands on, and that summary, which the state read shows — and moves nothing: it posts no Dispatch message, publishes nothing and starts no phase. Wherever the steps above say not to complete, do not call `handoff_complete`.

What ends your phase is the merge itself, as GitHub reports it: the daemon reads the merged pull request, from GitHub's webhook or from its own read of the issue, puts the issue in `verifying`, where its close waits for a pass of each check of its Verification document, and, where the project's `production_check` opens one, hands the production check to the tester's session, a fresh one where no step of the task was the tester's. It writes `done` once nothing holds that close. That is the one move forward out of `merging`, beside your own `request_backward_move` back, and it comes whether your completion arrived before it or after it. A root whose tree still runs waits in `verifying` on its merged pull request until the last other issue of its tree leaves the workflow, and closes then; a child with an issue beneath it still in the workflow waits there until the last of them is done or parked, and any other child closes once its own checks pass. An issue that reaches `merging` on a pull request GitHub has already merged is moved on at once too, so read the pull request before you merge: a merge someone else made is still the merge.

Your completion is refused on the grounds every phase worker's is, each by its code; a refusal records nothing, so the corrected call is applied. `HANDOFF_NOT_CURRENT_PHASE`: the issue is no longer in `merging` — report it to the architect and do not complete again. `HANDOFF_STALE_GENERATION`: your completion names a run the issue has left; push nothing further and tell the architect. `HANDOFF_NO_RUN`: your claim holds no task; tell the architect. `HANDOFF_ALREADY_RECORDED`: the first answer stands. `TREE_LINGERING`: the tree has left the workflow, and nothing of it is worked again. `HANDOFF_NOT_NEW` cannot reach you: `merging` is no file-backed phase, so you write no handoff record and nothing compares one with the record you named before.
