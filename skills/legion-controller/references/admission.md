# Reading admission

`read_state` says where each issue Legion records stands, and its `phase` alone does not: an issue
is `admitted` from the moment the daemon records it until its first phase starts, whether it runs,
waits, or neither. Read its key against `admission` and the root its own `tree` field names. A
slot is one issue the daemon has started, a root or a child, not one tree, so `admission.active`
no longer says which trees run: `admission.trees` does.

| What `read_state` shows | What it means | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| The key is in `admission.active` | An issue holding a slot, a root or a child: the daemon has started it and has not finished with it. An issue takes its slot when the daemon starts it and keeps it until it is done, leaves the workflow or waits only for its merge, so this list holds an issue at work, one in `accepting`, one `held`, one `merging` and one waiting on a person alike. One started issue is missing from here although the daemon runs it: one that waits on another issue, which returns its slot for that wait and is in `admission.waiting` and `admission.blocked` instead | Nothing, beyond [trees waiting on a root claim](skill://legion-controller/SKILL.md#trees-waiting-on-a-root-claim) |
| The key is in `admission.trees` | The root of a live tree: its lifecycle stands open and it does not linger, or it waits in the line or on a person. A tree is live whether or not any issue of it holds a slot, so this is the list that says which trees run, and `issues.<KEY>.tree` says which tree an issue stands in | Nothing: read it wherever you need the running trees |
| The key is in `admission.waiting` and not in `admission.blocked` | An issue standing in the line, in its own order: first the issues whose work came back, oldest first by when it came back — one owed its worker a start, which holds no slot and waits here for one, and two that hold the slot they were started with: a root a person answered in `awaiting_person`, and a tree's own root at `admitted` whose design gate has opened; then the issues whose first phase can start, by the Dispatch rank of their tree's root and then by their own. A root that left another tree (handed over by its finished tree, or admitted as an orphan) waits here as its own root even under a live tree, the old root's return included: no tree adopts it. Two kinds of that second part may hold a slot already: such a root, with the one it held in the tree it left, and a child the daemon started that a person set back to `todo`, with the one its run took. `issues.<KEY>.waitingLine` says where that issue stands, in its own fields: `part`, `returned` for an issue whose work came back or `starting` for one that has not started; `kind`, `returning` (a worker is owed a start), `answered` (a person answered the root), `queued_root` or `first_phase`; `partPlace` and `partLength`, its place among the issues of its own part and how many they are; `holdsSlot`, true where it already holds its admission slot and so takes no free one; `since`, the moment a `returned` issue's work came back; `rootRank`, `ownRank` and `rootKey`, the two Dispatch ranks that placed a `starting` issue and the key of its tree root; and `reason`, the daemon's own sentence for where it stands, which you quote in a report rather than write one of your own. Beside it, every issue that is not working now carries `why`, one answer with a `state` and a `reason` sentence: `claim_stopped`, a claim nothing will wake; `waiting`, this line; `blocked`, a Dispatch link or an open child below it; `unlisted`, the record holds it in the workflow and none of the three lists places it. An issue holding an admission slot carries no `why` unless it also stands in `admission.waiting` or `admission.blocked`, or a stranded claim names it: an answered root reads `waiting`, a root whose open children hold its own first phase reads `blocked`, and an issue at work whose worker runs, one `held` and a root whose ask a person has not answered yet, which `waitingOn` answers for, read nothing. Neither does an issue the record does not hold in the workflow (`done`, `backlog`, `icebox`, `triage`). The daemon narrows its answer to the contract the reading pod records: you read `partPlace` and every member above, and `why` with them, once your own pod runs a plugin at contract 85, and you read the `place` and `length` of today until then | Nothing: an issue holding a slot is promoted on the next pass whatever the cap says, and one holding none — an issue owed a start among them — waits here for the next free slot and takes it by itself. Read `reason` before you report an order as wrong: an issue of the second part stands behind every issue whose work came back, however it ranks ([Free slots](skill://legion-controller/SKILL.md#free-slots)) |
| `phase: awaiting_person`, `waitingOn` | A root that already ran and now waits only on a person: an answer to its architect's question (`waitingOn.kind` `ask`) or a review of its spec (`gate`). It keeps the slot it took when the daemon started it, its architect stays live to read the person's comments and replies, and its tree stays live. Unanswered, it is in `admission.active` and in no line; `waitingOn.answeredAt` means the daemon has put it in `admission.waiting` as well, among the issues whose work came back, oldest first, where it stands until the pass that resumes it | Nothing: it is not something to admit, label or park, and its wait frees no slot to take ([Free slots](skill://legion-controller/SKILL.md#free-slots)). The daemon delivers the answer task to its architect at that promotion, which takes no further slot, so it happens however full the cap is. If its label goes or a person or outside session claims it on Dispatch, the daemon sends `unslotted` and does not resume work under another owner |
| The key is in `admission.blocked` | An issue waiting on a Dispatch issue nobody has finished, with what it waits on: the Dispatch issues of its `blocked_by` links that nobody has finished, or, for a parent, the open children beneath it that children-first holds it for. An issue the daemon has not started yet takes no slot until they are done, and one that is in the line stands in `admission.waiting` too. An issue the daemon has started holds no admission slot while it waits either: it returns the slot it had, stands here and in `admission.waiting` among the issues whose work came back, oldest first, and takes a slot again by itself at the first pass after the wait ends. Nothing of its work stops meanwhile — no worker is suspended, no phase moves, no Dispatch status is written — and being in both lists and in neither `admission.active` is what tells it from an issue a person parked, which is in none of the three. Three started roots keep their slot although a wait names them (root `AGENTS.md`, "Admission" in the Issue Lifecycle section): a root in `awaiting_person`, a root at `admitted` whose design gate has never opened, and a root that arrived from another tree carrying the row it held there | Nothing: a link's wait ends when each blocker reaches `done` (a person's close, or the blocker's own merge) or its link is taken off (by a person or the tree's architect), and a children-first wait ends when the last open child leaves the workflow, which is its tree's architect's work. Name the wait in the day's report |
| `phase: admitted`, in neither `admission.active` nor `admission.waiting`, and Dispatch shows a `child_of` parent | A child that has not started: it runs or waits with its tree, the root its `child_of` chain reaches. Under a root whose `phase` is `done` it waits with it. A labelled `todo` issue here is not in the line either: one whose recorded parent is in a live tree runs in that tree (the daemon adopted it there), or is held with that parent while the parent itself is held under an unreleased child, and one whose parent is not recorded but sits under a live tree is held under an unreleased child of that tree until its architect releases it or its parent. One a person moved to `todo` beneath an ancestor whose reviewed head the daemon has already accepted is held here until that ancestor closes. The unreleased-child hold is not `admission.blocked`, which names blocked-by links and children-first waits | Leave it to its tree. It runs again when its root does, or as a root of its own once it is `todo` with the `legion` label and no live tree is above it |
| `phase: admitted`, `status: todo`, in neither list, no parent | A root a person took out of the line by taking its label off | Nothing: the label puts it back in the line, and a person's label is their decision |
| `phase: done` while `status` is `in_progress`, `testing`, `needs_review` or `retro` | Legion is not running it under that status, and it sent the `unslotted` wake. Either someone other than Legion moved a root that waited for a slot there, and the daemon took it out of the workflow at that move since no slot would ever reach it; or a root waiting on a person could not be resumed — its `legion` label is gone, or a person or an outside session took its Dispatch claim — and the daemon took it out the same way, leaving the `in_progress` it ran with. A parked child stays parked. Legion starts nothing for any of them | Re-admit nothing yourself: `dispatch read` shows who works it now. Name it in your summary; `todo` with the `legion` label queues it again |

A root left in the workflow at a status only a running tree has (`in_progress`, `testing`,
`needs_review`, `retro`) whose tree is not live, and which no blocker hold and no children-first
wait explains, does not stay that way: every pass of admission (a Dispatch
event, the daemon's boot read, its tick) takes it out of the workflow and sends the `unslotted`
wake — an unanswered `awaiting_person` root only when its label or its Dispatch claim makes a
resume unsafe. Holding no slot is by itself no fault: a root in `awaiting_merge`, a root waiting
in the line, a started issue that waits on another issue and a child its architect has not
released yet hold none while their tree runs. The
wake is live only, so a controller that starts later finds such a root by the last row, as its
start procedure does.
