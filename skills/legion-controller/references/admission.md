# Reading admission

`read_state` says where each issue Legion records stands, and its `phase` alone does not: an issue
is `admitted` from the moment the daemon records it until its first phase starts, whether it runs,
waits, or neither. Read its key against `admission` and the root its own `tree` field names. A
slot is one issue the daemon has started, a root or a child, not one tree, so `admission.active`
no longer says which trees run: `admission.trees` does.

| What `read_state` shows | What it means | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| The key is in `admission.active` | An issue holding a slot, a root or a child: the daemon has started it and has not finished with it. An issue takes its slot when the daemon starts it and keeps it until it is done, leaves the workflow or waits only for its merge, so this list holds an issue at work, one in `accepting`, one `held`, one `merging` and one waiting on a person alike | Nothing, beyond [trees waiting on a root claim](skill://legion-controller/SKILL.md#trees-waiting-on-a-root-claim) |
| The key is in `admission.trees` | The root of a live tree: its lifecycle stands open and it does not linger, or it waits in the line or on a person. A tree is live whether or not any issue of it holds a slot, so this is the list that says which trees run, and `issues.<KEY>.tree` says which tree an issue stands in | Nothing: read it wherever you need the running trees |
| The key is in `admission.waiting` | An issue that waits for a slot with nothing holding it, in the line's own order: first the issues whose work came back, oldest first by when it came back — one owed its worker a start, which holds no slot and waits here for one, and two that hold the slot they were started with: a root a person answered in `awaiting_person`, and a tree's own root at `admitted` whose design gate has opened; then the issues whose first phase can start, by the Dispatch rank of their tree's root and then by their own. A root that left another tree (handed over by its finished tree, or admitted as an orphan) waits here as its own root even under a live tree, the old root's return included: no tree adopts it. Two kinds of that second part may hold a slot already: such a root, with the one it held in the tree it left, and a child the daemon started that a person set back to `todo`, with the one its run took — each of those stands in `admission.active` rather than here, and keeps its place in the line, which is `issues.<KEY>.waitingLine` | Nothing: an issue holding a slot is promoted on the next pass whatever the cap says, and one holding none — an issue owed a start among them — waits here for the next free slot and takes it by itself |
| `phase: awaiting_person`, `waitingOn` | A root that already ran and now waits only on a person: an answer to its architect's question (`waitingOn.kind` `ask`) or a review of its spec (`gate`). It keeps the slot it took when the daemon started it, its architect stays live to read the person's comments and replies, and its tree stays live. Unanswered, it is in `admission.active` and in no line; `waitingOn.answeredAt` means the daemon has put it in the line, among the issues whose work came back, oldest first, where its `waitingLine` place stands until the pass that resumes it, while the list it is named in stays `admission.active`, since it holds the slot it took | Nothing: it is not something to admit, label or park, and its wait frees no slot to take ([Free slots](skill://legion-controller/SKILL.md#free-slots)). The daemon delivers the answer task to its architect at that promotion, which takes no further slot, so it happens however full the cap is. If its label goes or a person or outside session claims it on Dispatch, the daemon sends `unslotted` and does not resume work under another owner |
| The key is in `admission.blocked` | An issue Legion is waiting to start, with what it waits for. Either `blockedBy`, the Dispatch issues of its `blocked_by` links that nobody has finished or, for a parent, the open children beneath it that children-first holds it for; or `wait`, one hold of the daemon's own with the issue it turns on — `tree_closed` (its tree has closed and owes it a hand-over), `design_gate` (a person has not approved the tree's design), `awaiting_person` (the root has asked a person and has no answer), `accepted_ancestor` (an ancestor the daemon has accepted must close first), `unreleased_work` (its chain reaches another tree, whose architect releases it) or `unlabelled` (a person has taken the label off the root, which names no issue). An issue named here is in `admission.waiting` no more, and takes no slot until the hold ends; one that already holds a slot is in `admission.active` beside this, which says the daemon started it and this says what its next phase waits for | Nothing: a link's wait ends when each blocker reaches `done` (a person's close, or the blocker's own merge) or its link is taken off (by a person or the tree's architect), and a children-first wait ends when the last open child leaves the workflow, which is its tree's architect's work. A hold of the daemon's own ends with the move its `wait.kind` names, every one of which is a person's or an architect's. Name the wait in the day's report |
| `phase: admitted`, in `admission.blocked` and in neither `admission.active` nor `admission.waiting`, and Dispatch shows a `child_of` parent | A child that has not started: it runs or waits with its tree, the root its `child_of` chain reaches. Under a root whose `phase` is `done` it waits with it. A labelled `todo` issue here is not in the line either: one whose recorded parent is in a live tree runs in that tree (the daemon adopted it there), or is held with that parent while the parent itself is held under an unreleased child, and one whose parent is not recorded but sits under a live tree is held under an unreleased child of that tree until its architect releases it or its parent. One a person moved to `todo` beneath an ancestor whose reviewed head the daemon has already accepted is held here until that ancestor closes. Each of those is named in `admission.blocked` under its own `wait.kind` rather than as a blocked-by link | Leave it to its tree. It runs again when its root does, or as a root of its own once it is `todo` with the `legion` label and no live tree is above it |
| `phase: admitted`, `status: todo`, no parent, in `admission.blocked` under `wait.kind` `unlabelled` | A root a person took out of the line by taking its label off | Nothing: the label puts it back in the line, and a person's label is their decision |
| `phase: done` while `status` is `in_progress`, `testing`, `needs_review` or `retro` | Legion is not running it under that status, and it sent the `unslotted` wake. Either someone other than Legion moved a root that waited for a slot there, and the daemon took it out of the workflow at that move since no slot would ever reach it; or a root waiting on a person could not be resumed — its `legion` label is gone, or a person or an outside session took its Dispatch claim — and the daemon took it out the same way, leaving the `in_progress` it ran with. A parked child stays parked. Legion starts nothing for any of them | Re-admit nothing yourself: `dispatch read` shows who works it now. Name it in your summary; `todo` with the `legion` label queues it again |

A root left in the workflow at a status only a running tree has (`in_progress`, `testing`,
`needs_review`, `retro`) whose tree is not live, and which no blocker hold and no children-first
wait explains, does not stay that way: every pass of admission (a Dispatch
event, the daemon's boot read, its tick) takes it out of the workflow and sends the `unslotted`
wake — an unanswered `awaiting_person` root only when its label or its Dispatch claim makes a
resume unsafe. Holding no slot is by itself no fault: a root in `awaiting_merge`, a root waiting
in the line and a child its architect has not released yet hold none while their tree runs. The
wake is live only, so a controller that starts later finds such a root by the last row, as its
start procedure does.
