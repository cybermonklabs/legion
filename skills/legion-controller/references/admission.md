# Reading admission

Read this when you need to know where an issue stands in admission, or what an `admission.held` word means. An issue's `phase` alone does not say it: read its key against the `admission` lists and the root its `tree` names. The rules behind them: [Admission and the `legion` label](https://cybermonklabs.github.io/legion/legion/concepts/#admission-and-the-legion-label).

## The lists

- `admission.active`: every issue holding a slot, a root or a child, at work, `held` or waiting on a person. An issue past its review (`accepting`, `retro`, `merging`) holds none, and neither does a started issue that waits on another issue, except three roots that keep theirs: one in `awaiting_person`, one at `admitted` whose gate has never opened, and one carried from another tree.
- `admission.trees`: the roots of the live trees, whether or not any issue of them holds a slot. Use it, not `admission.active`, for which trees run.
- `admission.waiting`: the line, in order. First the issues whose work came back, oldest first by when it came back: one owed its worker a start, and one `waiting_on_issue`, a started issue that gave its slot back for a wait, which hold no slot and wait here for one; and two that hold the slot they were started with: a root a person answered in `awaiting_person`, and a tree's own root at `admitted` whose design gate has opened. Then the issues whose first phase can start, by their root's Dispatch rank and then their own. Two kinds of that second part may hold a slot already: a root that left another tree, with the one it held in the tree it left, and a child the daemon started that a person set back to `todo`, with the one its run took. A root that left another tree (handed over by its finished tree, or admitted as an orphan) waits here as its own root even under a live tree, the old root's return included: no tree adopts it.
- `issues.<KEY>.waitingLine` says where an issue of the line stands, in its own fields: `part`, `returned` for an issue whose work came back or `starting` for one that has not started; `kind`, `returning` (a worker is owed a start), `answered` (a person answered the root), `queued_root`, `first_phase` or `waiting_on_issue` (a started issue that gave its slot back to wait on another issue); `partPlace` and `partLength`, its place among the issues of its own part and how many they are; `holdsSlot`, true where it already holds its admission slot and so takes no free one; `since`, the moment a `returned` issue's work came back; `rootRank`, `ownRank` and `rootKey`, the two Dispatch ranks that placed a `starting` issue and the key of its tree root; and `reason`, the daemon's own sentence for where it stands, which you quote in a report rather than write one of your own. Read `reason` before you report an order as wrong: an issue of the second part stands behind every issue whose work came back, however it ranks.
- `issues.<KEY>.why`: every issue that is not working now carries one answer, a `state` and a `reason` sentence: `claim_stopped`, a claim nothing will wake; `waiting`, this line; `blocked`, a Dispatch link or an open child below it; `unlisted`, the record holds it in the workflow and none of the three lists places it. An issue holding an admission slot carries no `why` unless it also stands in `admission.waiting` or `admission.blocked`, or its claim has stopped: an answered root reads `waiting`, and an issue at work whose worker runs, one `held` and a root whose ask a person has not answered yet, which `waitingOn` answers for, read nothing. Neither does an issue the record does not hold in the workflow (`done`, `backlog`, `icebox`, `triage`).
- You read `partPlace` and every field after it, and `why` with them, once your own pod runs a plugin at contract 85; until then the daemon serves `waitingLine` with `place` and `length` alone.
- `admission.blocked`: issues waiting on an unfinished `blocked_by` issue, or a parent waiting for its open children. A started issue here gave its slot back, stands in `admission.waiting` too, and takes one again by itself; nothing of its work stops. Being in both lists and not in `admission.active` tells it from an issue a person parked, which is in none of the three.
- `phase: awaiting_person` with `waitingOn`: a root waiting on a person's answer or review keeps its slot and is in no line. `waitingOn.answeredAt` means the daemon has put it in `admission.waiting` as well, among the issues whose work came back, oldest first, where it stands until the pass that resumes it.
- `phase: admitted`, in no list, with a `child_of` parent: a child that has not started. Leave it to its tree. A labelled `todo` issue here is not in the line either: one whose recorded parent is in a live tree runs in that tree (the daemon adopted it there), or is held with that parent while the parent itself is held under an unreleased child; one whose parent is not recorded but sits under a live tree is held under an unreleased child of that tree until an architect of that tree above it releases it or its parent. One a person moved to `todo` beneath an ancestor whose reviewed head the daemon has already accepted is held here until that ancestor closes. The unreleased-child hold is not `admission.blocked`, which names `blocked_by` links and children-first waits.
- `phase: admitted`, `status: todo`, in no list, no parent: a person took its label off. Nothing to do.
- `phase: done` while `status` is `in_progress`, `testing`, `needs_review` or `retro`: someone else moved a waiting root, or a root waiting on a person lost its label or claim, and the daemon sent `unslotted`. Re-admit nothing.

Holding no slot is no fault by itself.
