# Reading admission

`read_state` says where each issue Legion records stands, and its `phase` alone does not: an issue
is `admitted` from the moment the daemon records it until its first phase starts, whether it runs,
waits, or neither. Read its key against `admission` and the root its own `tree` field names. A
slot is one issue the daemon has started, a root or a child, not one tree, so `admission.active`
no longer says which trees run: `admission.trees` does.

| What `read_state` shows | What it means | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| The key is in `admission.active` | An issue holding a slot, a root or a child: the daemon has started it and has not finished with it. An issue takes its slot when the daemon starts it and keeps it until it is done, leaves the workflow or waits only for its merge, so this list holds an issue at work, one in `accepting`, one `held`, one `merging` and one waiting on a person alike | Nothing, beyond [trees waiting on a root claim](skill://legion-controller/SKILL.md#trees-waiting-on-a-root-claim) |
| The key is in `admission.trees` | The root of a live tree: its lifecycle stands open and it does not linger, or it waits in the line or on a person. A tree is live whether or not any issue of it holds a slot, so this is the list that says which trees run, and `issues.<KEY>.tree` says which tree an issue stands in | Nothing: read it wherever you need the running trees |
| The key is in `admission.waiting` and not in `admission.blocked` | An issue standing in the line, in its own order: first the issues whose work came back, oldest first by when it came back — one owed its worker a start, which holds no slot and waits here for one, and two that hold the slot they were started with: a root a person answered in `awaiting_person`, and a tree's own root at `admitted` whose design gate has opened; then the issues whose first phase can start, by the Dispatch rank of their tree's root and then by their own. A root that left another tree (handed over by its finished tree, or admitted as an orphan) waits here as its own root even under a live tree, the old root's return included: no tree adopts it. Two kinds of that second part may hold a slot already: such a root, with the one it held in the tree it left, and a child the daemon started that a person set back to `todo`, with the one its run took | Nothing: an issue holding a slot is promoted on the next pass whatever the cap says, and one holding none — an issue owed a start among them — waits here for the next free slot and takes it by itself |
| `phase: awaiting_person`, `waitingOn` | A root that already ran and now waits only on a person: an answer to its architect's question (`waitingOn.kind` `ask`) or a review of its spec (`gate`). It keeps the slot it took when the daemon started it, its architect stays live to read the person's comments and replies, and its tree stays live. Unanswered, it is in `admission.active` and in no line; `waitingOn.answeredAt` means the daemon has put it in `admission.waiting` as well, among the issues whose work came back, oldest first, where it stands until the pass that resumes it | Nothing: it is not something to admit, label or park, and its wait frees no slot to take ([Free slots](skill://legion-controller/SKILL.md#free-slots)). The daemon delivers the answer task to its architect at that promotion, which takes no further slot, so it happens however full the cap is. If its label goes or a person or outside session claims it on Dispatch, the daemon sends `unslotted` and does not resume work under another owner |
| The key is in `admission.blocked` | An issue Legion is waiting to start, with what it waits on: the Dispatch issues of its `blocked_by` links that nobody has finished, or, for a parent, the open children beneath it that children-first holds it for. It takes no slot until they are done, and one that is in the line stands in `admission.waiting` too | Nothing: a link's wait ends when each blocker reaches `done` (a person's close, or the blocker's own merge) or its link is taken off (by a person or the tree's architect), and a children-first wait ends when the last open child leaves the workflow, which is its tree's architect's work. Name the wait in the day's report |
| `phase: admitted`, in neither `admission.active` nor `admission.waiting`, and Dispatch shows a `child_of` parent | A child that has not started: it runs or waits with its tree, the root its `child_of` chain reaches. Under a root whose `phase` is `done` it waits with it. A labelled `todo` issue here is not in the line either: one whose recorded parent is in a live tree runs in that tree (the daemon adopted it there), or is held with that parent while the parent itself is held under an unreleased child, and one whose parent is not recorded but sits under a live tree is held under an unreleased child of that tree until its architect releases it or its parent. One a person moved to `todo` beneath an ancestor whose reviewed head the daemon has already accepted is held here until that ancestor closes. The unreleased-child hold is not `admission.blocked`, which names blocked-by links and children-first waits | Leave it to its tree. It runs again when its root does, or as a root of its own once it is `todo` with the `legion` label and no live tree is above it |
| `phase: admitted`, `status: todo`, in neither list, no parent | A root a person took out of the line by taking its label off | Nothing: the label puts it back in the line, and a person's label is their decision |
| `phase: done` while `status` is `in_progress`, `testing`, `needs_review` or `retro` | Legion is not running it under that status, and it sent the `unslotted` wake. Either someone other than Legion moved a root that waited for a slot there, and the daemon took it out of the workflow at that move since no slot would ever reach it; or a root waiting on a person could not be resumed — its `legion` label is gone, or a person or an outside session took its Dispatch claim — and the daemon took it out the same way, leaving the `in_progress` it ran with. A parked child stays parked. Legion starts nothing for any of them | Re-admit nothing yourself: `dispatch read` shows who works it now. Name it in your summary; `todo` with the `legion` label queues it again |

A root left in the workflow at a status only a running tree has (`in_progress`, `testing`,
`needs_review`, `retro`) whose tree is not live, and which no blocker hold and no children-first
wait explains, does not stay that way: every pass of admission (a Dispatch
event, the daemon's boot read, its tick) takes it out of the workflow and sends the `unslotted`
wake — an unanswered `awaiting_person` root only when its label or its Dispatch claim makes a
resume unsafe. Holding no slot is by itself no fault: a root in `awaiting_merge`, a root waiting
in the line and a child its architect has not released yet hold none while their tree runs. The
wake is live only, so a controller that starts later finds such a root by the last row, as its
start procedure does.

## `admission.held`: what nothing starts

`admission.held` is every issue the daemon's record holds in the workflow that stands in neither
`admission.active` nor `admission.waiting`. Each row carries the issue, one word for why nothing
of it runs, and `heldBy`, the issues that hold it, which is absent where no issue holds it. The
daemon reads the words in the order of the table below and takes the first that is true, so one
issue reads one word, and that word is the same in `read_state` and in the `tick` notice. The
list names no issue `admission.active` or `admission.waiting` names, so the three lists together
are every issue of the workflow and what each one is doing. `admission.blocked` keeps its own
rows beside them: it names Dispatch links and children-first waits alone, and an issue it names
that stands in `admission.held` reads there under whichever word the daemon's order gives it,
which can be an earlier one — a child under a shut gate that a link also holds reads
`design_gate`. A blocked root the line carries stands in `admission.waiting` and has no held row
at all.

The daemon starts none of these issues by itself. Name them in the day's report and take nothing
for them, except where the row's last column names an act of yours.

| Word | What it means | What ends the hold |
|---|---|---|
| `tree_closed` | The issue's tree root carries the Dispatch status `done`, or that root's linger is still running after it left the workflow. `heldBy` names that root | An architect's `release_children` of the live tree whose Dispatch parent the issue reaches: the daemon records it in that tree at `admitted`, with its repository kept and its generation raised by one. The row alone does not say whether that release would be accepted, and two kinds of issue read this word that no release takes: a member of a tree still in its linger, and a member of a tree a person parked, whose park starts a linger of its own. Both are refused while that linger runs |
| `tree_parked` | The issue's tree root left the workflow to `backlog` or `icebox`, because a person parked that tree, and that root's linger has run out. While it still runs the member reads `tree_closed` instead. `heldBy` names that root | A person's `todo` on the root, which re-admits the tree, and nothing else: no release takes such a member. Where the park is your own hold, the parked-root walk decides it (`skill://legion-controller/references/parked-decisions.md`) |
| `label_off` | The issue is a root the admission queue does not carry, because the record says the `legion` label is off. A person took it out of Legion | A person's label. Take no issue back yourself: the intake rule in your deployment instructions keeps such a root out |
| `ancestry` | The root's Dispatch chain reaches another live root, so the issue is that tree's unreleased work. `heldBy` names the live roots on that chain | That tree's architect releasing the issue, or releasing the parent above it; or every live root leaving that chain, after which it queues as a root of its own |
| `root_awaiting_person` | The issue's tree root waits on a person's answer or on a review of its spec, so nothing of the tree starts. `heldBy` names that root | The person's answer or review. The daemon then resumes the root's architect, and the tree's issues start with it |
| `design_gate` | The design gate of the issue's tree root is not open at every step the deployment gates. `heldBy` names that root | A person's approval of each gated step's document at its current version |
| `dispatch_blocker` | A Dispatch `blocked_by` link of the issue names an issue that is not `done`, and holds the issue where it stands. `heldBy` names the unfinished blockers | Each blocker reaching `done` (a person's close, or the blocker's own merge), or the link taken off by a person or by the tree's architect. The issue is in `admission.blocked` as well |
| `children_first` | Dispatch shows an open child under the issue, so its own first phase waits for the children. `heldBy` names the open children | The last open child leaving the workflow, which is the tree architect's work. The issue is in `admission.blocked` as well |
| `accepted_ancestor` | The daemon has accepted the reviewed head of an issue above this one, so this one starts at that ancestor's close. `heldBy` names that ancestor | The ancestor's close, which the merge of its pull request writes |
| `at_work` | The issue stands in a phase that holds no admission slot and that the line does not carry, which is `awaiting_merge`. Its workers run as they did, and the word says nothing about them | The merge of the issue's pull request, which closes the issue. No `heldBy`: no other issue holds it |
| `unclassified` | No word above is true of the issue. The daemon logs it once for that issue and run | Nothing of yours. Name it in the day's report with its `phase` and `status`, so a person can read what the daemon could not say |
