Shared TypeScript HTTP layer and `dispatch` command for every agent host: Dispatch config resolution, command execution, the text of each result (`dispatch search` prints each ask hit's answer state under it), inbound envelope rendering, the list of what people wrote to a session in the last 72 hours (`src/session-answers.ts`), which every host carries on each model request within an 800 ms deadline from one process-wide read and a memory and disk cache, and which `dispatch answers` prints in full, the forms in which a write, or `dispatch related`, prints the related-item check's result, the relay protocol of consumer delivery (`consumer-delivery`: `received` at decode, `done` once the host wrote the frame, the resume point), and the ledger that keeps a session from being shown a frame twice (`delivery-ledger`). It also holds the NATS connection options every session plugin dials with (`nats-connection`: a ping every 10 s, a path dropped after two unanswered pings, so a frozen path is left within 30 s; reconnect forever, 2 s apart, or, for a session that presents its broker token, at once after a connection that lived a minute past its last dial and otherwise where its backoff stands, then after jittered waits doubling from 2-4 s to 10-20 s, with a token it has yet to present sent within about a second, mid-wait too: a switch's whatever the wait, a renewal's at most once per wait until a connection is admitted, `brokerRedialDelays`; a refused first dial waits the same, and drops a token refused twice, once for the session until a dial is admitted, on the count the broker credential keeps and its keeper shares, `connectNats`, `BrokerNatsCredential.refused`), and the session registration, whose `subscribe()` sends the plugin's npm package name and build as `plugin` and `plugin_version` on every registration.

`subscribe()` also sends the host's client instance id as `client_instance_id`, and `unregisterSession({sessionID, clientInstanceID?})` sends it as a query parameter, so the listener removes a registration only while it carries that id or none. The registration is also the holder's heartbeat for its role claims, and the listener renews every unmarked claim that registration's own process holds whatever topics the registration carries; one that states no instance renews the claims of its session that name no process and adopts none, so the claim of a client that cannot say which process it is stays renewed while that client runs, and once it has, no registration that states an instance adopts that claim. `setRole` and `unsubscribe` take a `clientInstanceID` of their own, sent as `client_instance_id` and omitted from the wire when the caller states none: the claim records that process as the role's holder, and a release frees only the role claims naming it — a claim that names no process is freed by no release but the administrator's force — so a second process that resumed this session id can neither take the role nor hand it back. The unsubscribe answer's `removed` is the topics that left the record rather than the ones the request named: a role lane topic whose claim the release did not free stays in the record, because one record serves every process running under one session id. A release that states no process and names a role's own lane topic is a hand-back, and the listener marks the claim it could not free, whether that claim names a process or none. A release the holder's own renewal beat under each of the route's three conditional deletes fails rather than answering success, so `unsubscribe` rejects and the caller hands the role back again rather than reading it as given up. A `setRole` soft claim naming `previousSessionID` takes the role from that predecessor only when the predecessor has no live registry entry, when its live entry carries this client instance's own id, or when either side carries no instance id. A soft claim of a role already held under the claimant's own session id is decided on the standing claim: granted when that claim names this very process, granted when nobody renewed it inside the listener's live window, and otherwise answered `{claimed: false, holder}` naming that same session. Any other live holder keeps the role and is answered `{claimed: false, holder}`. A role lookup's `RoleInfo` carries the holder's `client_instance_id` where the claim names a process and leaves the field out where it names none, which is how a refused claimant tells its own session id held by another process from a different session, and `handed_back` where the claim carries the listener's hand-back mark. The two rules every client keeps over those ids are exported beside `RoleInfo`: `roleHeldBy(info, sessionID, clientInstanceID)`, whether a lookup's answer is this caller's own hold of the role, and `mayActOnRoleRecord(writer, clientInstanceID, holdsRole)`, whether a persisted role record another process may have written is this one's to act on. Both read an empty id as no process named, exactly as an absent one. A marked claim is no hold of anyone's, whoever `roleHeldBy` is asked for: the mark says the role was handed back and nothing renews the claim any more, so a holder whose claim names its process and an older client's hand-back marked claims the role again at its next heartbeat and has it back, rather than reading the lookup as confirmation until the reaper frees it. A soft claim over a marked claim that names no process is weighed by that claim's renewal and refused until it is older than the listener's live window or the reaper frees the claim; an outright `setRole` (no `soft`) takes it back at once (`packages/envoy-client/README.md`).

A session's credential is a bearer or an access token traded at the secrets broker. `src/bearer.ts` holds both as one `RequestCredential` — a scheme and the headers one request presents — and `src/broker-credential.ts` makes the second out of `@legion/cipher-client`: one client per session a process acts as, shared by that session's Dispatch client and listener client, presented as `Authorization: DPoP <token>` with a proof naming each request and hashing the token it goes beside. With no sign-in on the machine the client trades this machine's own identity, `kubernetes`, `metadata` or `pool`, with a key the process makes, under the session's own name. Where a person ran `agent-secrets login` on the machine it trades that person, with the machine as the actor and the sign-in's box key, so the token names the person and Dispatch gives it the `person` scope and never `approve`. While a sign-in record is on the machine it never trades the machine in its place: an error fails the call, and a final refusal of that sign-in is kept for the life of the process and refused again with no network call until the store holds another sign-in. The one exception is a process whose environment sets `PERSON_SIGNIN_TRADE=off`, which trades the machine even where a person signed in; `legion controller start` sets it for the controller under every configuration, so the controller's own Dispatch and Envoy credentials never trade its operator's sign-in, while its `agent-secrets` secret reads are signed as they would be without it. The broker route is the last source either section consults, reached only when no bearer is configured at all (`DISPATCH_TOKEN`, `DISPATCH_TOKEN_FILE`, `ENVOY_TOKEN`, `ENVOY_TOKEN_FILE` and an envoy.json's own token are resolved first), so a session that has a bearer presents that bearer. Dispatch's section has one exception, `DISPATCH_TOKEN_BROKER=1`: a deployment sets it on a session whose Dispatch credential must be traded at the broker and nothing else (`legion controller start` under `dispatch_token_broker`, where the chosen envoy.json is the operator's own and holds that person's `dispatch.token`), and under it no bearer is read from any source, while a session that can trade none turns Dispatch off with an error rather than falling back to a person's token. Any other value of that variable is refused by name. A resolution holds that credential as its one `credential` field (`ActiveDispatchConfig`, `EnabledEnvoySettings`), and `DispatchClient` takes only a `RequestCredential`, so no caller can send a bare `Bearer ` to Dispatch. Each write attempt presents the credential afresh, since a resource server that keeps a proof replay store refuses one it has already seen. When Dispatch answers `401 BROKER_TOKEN_NOT_ACTIVE`, for a broker token a revoke ended before it lapsed, `DispatchClient` tells the credential (`stale`), presents it again, which trades afresh, and sends the request once more, a write under the same `Idempotency-Key`. `dispatch review` records a person's review of one document version (`POST /api/v1/artifacts/{id}/reviews` with the `version` the caller read), so it works only with a token that names a person who holds `approve`: a personal token the person gave `approve`, since no broker token, a sign-in's included, holds it.

Two things the `dispatch` command prints come from the version a write names and the advice the server answers with. `dispatch artifact` sends the token of the version an upload replaces: the `precondition` object rides the JSON body of an inline upload and the multipart form field of that name of a `--path` upload (`src/dispatch-http.ts`'s `#upload`), so both encodings reach the route that reads them, and Dispatch's refusal of a token behind the live document reaches the reader in Dispatch's own words, which name `dispatch doc-read` and say to upload again. `dispatch doc-read` of an uploaded file or picture (`readUploadedFile` in `src/dispatch-commands/documents.ts`) names the version it read by its hash on the header's second line, before the file's text, so the text after the header is the stored file unchanged, a leading byte order mark included, except for what the `dispatch` command does to every output: it adds a line feed after a file that ends without one, and under Claude Code or Codex it cuts an output past 25,000 characters and writes the whole of it to a file it names (`fitOutput` in `src/dispatch-cli.ts`). The line is the latest version's `Document token:` line, from the `token` Dispatch states for it, or a named version's `Content: sha256:` line, from its `sha256`, both read off the artifact's version list, so it makes no other read; a Dispatch or a version that states none prints no line. The command's refusal of an upload under a name the owner holds names only what Dispatch takes and prints no token, so a writer names the version its own read printed (`artifactNameExistsProblem` in `src/dispatch-commands/upload-checks.ts`, its words `fileNameExistsRule` and `artifactKindConflictRule` in `@legion/contracts`): for an upload of another kind than the name holds, decided by Dispatch's rule (`uploadKindOf`: inline content and a Markdown path are a document, a path whose first 14 bytes, read by `readPictureHead`, `sniffPictureType` reads as a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP by Go's `http.DetectContentType` signatures a picture, anything else a file), another name; for a file or a picture of its kind, `--force` with the token the writer's own `dispatch doc-read` printed, or `--force` alone where the newest version states no token. `dispatch ask` prints the review the issue's specification already carries (`WriteAdvice.standing_review`) between the line naming the ask, with the related-item check's own lines under it, and the line saying the sender follows the ask: who reviewed, at which version, when, whether they approved or asked for changes, the reason in quotes or that they gave none, and one line asking the sender to say what changed since that review. A carried review says it was carried forward, names the version its carry reason gives rather than the version it sits on, and prints no reason, the stored one being Dispatch's own carry sentence. `renderAdvice`'s cadence line stays the result's last line.

`src/related-check.ts` is the text a write, and `dispatch related`, print for the related-item check. The lines hold exactly one of three forms, and no line says that an earlier answer settles the question asked. Where the server sends `other_projects`, the answered questions of other projects print after every item of the asking project, under the heading `Answered questions in other projects:`, and each row names the project its item is in. The heading says where the items come from and claims nothing more. The reader decides what they answer. A server that sends none prints no heading, and a field this client no longer reads is ignored.
