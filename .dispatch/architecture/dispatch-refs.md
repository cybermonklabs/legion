Mentions, structural containment, and the typed edges of `graph_edges` — `mentions`, `child_of`, `blocked_by`, `attached_to`, `anchored_to`, `owned_by`, `replies_to`, `followed_by`, plus the `part_of`, `depends_on` and `affects` edges of the architecture model itself. `rebuild.go` is the reconciliation job that rebuilds the graph from the rows it indexes.

Most of refs works inside a caller's transaction, and a write path's reads and writes are two halves the caller runs in its own two phases: `PlanReplace` and `PlanReplaceFrom` read the edges a source holds and plan the change, and `Replacement.Queue`, `ItemIndexing.Queue`, `BlockIndexing.Queue`, `QueueRecordExcerpts` and `ClearExcerpts` put the writes on the caller's write-phase batch (`store.WriteBatch`), so a writer that decides everything first plans in its read phase and writes in its write phase, after the owner's advisory lock it took first. `replaceRefs` runs its plan and its queue back to back as a batch of its own (`store.RunWrite`) for the callers that write nothing else. The background passes open their own transactions and take the owner's lock themselves: `ReindexOwed`, `ReindexScopeSource` and `IndexOwedItemBlocks` read the source under its owner's lock (`readSourceUnderOwner`, `store.LockOwnerAndRead`), plan with no transaction id held, and write their batch, the owed row's delete first where there is one; the pass that writes an item's missing windows, and docs' anchor-block backfill, take the item's owner lock through `LockItemOwner` (`store.LockOwner`). A source with no owner, a direct message, is read with no lock. So refs imports the store for its owner locks and its write batch, and its own SQL takes no row lock of an owner table (beside an earlier build, the owner locks it takes lock the owners' rows too: `dispatch-store`).

`LockItemOwner` takes an advisory lock and no row lock, so it excludes exactly the writers that take the same lock: it rests on every writer of a comment, a message, an ask or one of their block-state rows taking that item's owner lock first. `api` holds that in two halves: a census of the names of every such writer (`TestEveryWriterOfAnItemRowIsOnTheOwnerLockCensus`) and a lock table that holds each serving writer behind the lock (`TestEveryWriterOnTheLockTableWaitsForTheOwnerLockOfTheRowItWrites`), with every census name on the table or in its gap map. The writers that take no owner lock are named exceptions: `cmd/dispatch-seed`'s three item loaders, which fill a database before any server is started on it; `going/eval`'s three seed writers, which write only items on issues and documents their own transaction inserts into a database holding no project, so no other writer sees those owners until it commits; `api`'s `writeBroadcast`, whose direct messages have no owner; docs' `restoreWordings`, which runs once at boot under the copy locks alone; and `RebuildAll`, which raises only the owed markers of every comment, message and ask.
