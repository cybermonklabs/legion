The publisher that turns each appended event into deliveries: its owner's topic, its route and its author and follower routes. One process publishes at a time, under a hold it renews. It sends each owner's events to each destination in order, retries a failed destination with exponential backoff, and records what a batch came to in one statement. It is the one package of the server that imports the generated Go contracts, because the envelope it publishes is the contract. Each destination it reaches is appended to the event's `published_destinations`, and that record is a contract of its own: besides the retry, which publishes only the destinations not yet recorded, `GET /api/v1/agents/{session_id}/answers` (`sessionanswers.List`) lists a person's event for a session exactly when that session's own topic is recorded there. A destination NATS refuses for good is not recorded there: it goes into `refused_destinations` with its error (Failure classes, below), and the retry skips it too. A change to which routes reach a session, or to what is recorded, changes what every agent is shown on its next model request.

## One publisher, and take-over

Every envoy-dispatch process runs an outbox (`outbox.New`, `Run`). Only the process holding the publisher hold reads and sends events: `dispatch-outbox`, a `duty.Hold` row in `duty_holds` (migration `20261101143201_duty_holds`). The hold lasts 6 s on the database's clock after each renewal, is renewed every 1 s, and reports itself lost 2 s before it lapses on the process's monotonic clock. So the window a renewal has is 4 s from the last one that was confirmed, each renewal's own wait for an answer is bounded at the renewal interval, and the window therefore holds three usable attempts, at 1 s, 2 s and 3 s; at the 2 s renewal interval the hold had before, it held one, and one statement that did not answer within 2 s cost a hold whose row was still live. The hold is taken and renewed on the outbox's own pool (`store.Pool.Outbox`, two connections), so a renewal never waits behind a batch statement or behind the API's requests on the shared pool. The batches running side by side run one statement at a time on that pool ("Owner lanes" below), so one of its two connections is always the renewal's. The others stand by.

- **Killed holder.** Its row lapses 6 s after its last renewal. A standby arms one timer to that moment (`duty.Expiry` reads what is left on the database's clock), takes the hold when the timer fires, and scans at once.
- **Stopping holder.** It takes no further batch. In each batch in flight it makes no further send once its context ends, settles the sends still in flight as lost, records what NATS already answered and releases the rest, and then releases the hold, once every batch has returned. The release deletes the row and sends `pg_notify('dispatch_duty_holds', 'dispatch-outbox')` in one statement, which wakes a standby at once. A graceful stop of `envoy-dispatch` waits for both before it closes the database pool, for at most `outboxStopBudget` after its shutdown starts (`cmd/dispatch/shutdown.go`).
- **Holder without NATS.** It releases the hold when its client has been disconnected for 3 s without a break and still reads disconnected when the 3 s end: the grace timer reads `Connected()` again, because a reconnect's event can wait in nats.go's callback queue behind a slow callback, and a client connected again keeps the hold and scans. It also releases the hold when a batch's sends fail for the connection (`bus.ErrUnavailable`, or `bus.ErrNotAcknowledged` on a connection still current) and a 2 s flush probe of that window's connection fails too. That probe is what finds a silent cut, a connection that still reports itself connected. A client that has already reconnected since the batch began keeps the hold and resends on the new connection. A send that waits for a connection reads it again at each connection event and at least every second (`bus.reconnectRecheck`), for the same queued event.
- **Standby.** It tries the hold only while its own NATS client is connected. A process that gave the hold up for NATS loss also first needs an answered probe of its connection before it tries again, so it does not take back the hold it gave up for that same silent connection ahead of a standby whose NATS works. If both processes lose NATS, neither holds until one reconnects.
- **Holder whose renewal was not confirmed in time.** It is alive, and it stops publishing at its loss deadline, 4 s after the last confirmed renewal: `Publishing()` turns false there and `/healthz` says so at once, and the holder starts no batch and completes no send past it (Fencing, below). Its own row lapses 2 s later, the hold's margin, and no standby may take the name while that row stands - the margin is what keeps a holder that stops ahead of any process that starts. The row stands until the holder's release deletes it, right after the line that ends the turn, or until it lapses when that release cannot reach the database first; a standby waits for the earlier of the two. The same process may take the name again sooner, because `takeHold` admits the row's own holder whether or not it has lapsed. The line that ends the turn (`lost the publisher hold`) is written before the release, which is a database round trip, and carries two bounds on the moment the process stopped publishing: `stopped`, the earlier of the hold's deadline and a clock read taken before the holder cleared its hold, which is at or before that moment, and `stopped_by`, the earlier of the hold's deadline and a clock read taken after it cleared it, which is at or after it. Two, because a record that found the hold another's ends the turn with the deadline still ahead, where the stop is the clearing of the hold itself and a single clock read would put a reader on the wrong side of it; on the paths that end at the deadline both are that deadline, so the stop is exact. Read the gap from `stopped` and `stopped_by` and not from the line's own time. The line that begins a turn (`took the publisher hold`) carries `started`, read before the process began publishing, since the line's own time is written to the millisecond and after the start.

`/healthz` reports `outbox_publisher`: `true` while this process holds the hold and its loss deadline has not passed, and `false` on a standby, after a loss, and with NATS disabled. It never decides `ok`.

## Fencing

A process publishes only while it holds the hold and its loss deadline has not passed. A batch sends through a window that carries the deadline (`bus.Client.WindowUntil`), and the deadline governs those sends. The window checks it before anything else of each send, caps a send's wait for a connection at it, and checks it again as the last act before the write: a send at or past the deadline writes nothing and answers `bus.ErrWindowDeadline`, which the batch reads as fenced. So a process frozen past its deadline (SIGSTOP, a long pause) sends nothing when it resumes, and a batch one of whose sends met the deadline is left unrecorded for the next holder. A batch whose sends all went out under the hold records what they came to however long their answers took, the deadline passing while it waited for them included: the hold covers the publish window it opened, and each answer is to a send the hold itself made. The deadline is Margin ahead of the row's lapse, so the row is still this holder's whenever the check passes. The record writes what is true whoever holds the hold, regardless of the hold: destinations reached, and `published_at` once every destination is reached. It writes back-offs, hold-backs and releases only while the hold row still names this holder and has not lapsed, checked in the same statement. A record that finds the hold another's ends the holder as one that lost the hold. A lost hold is released like any other: the release stops its renewal, and its delete and notify are guarded on this holder, so another's row is left alone. A copy an old holder sent before it stopped lands as a duplicate, never ahead of a later event of its destination, because the new holder sends that event again first.

## Wake

Nothing scans on a period. The holder and the standby have one one-slot wake channel each. The holder's is signalled by:

- a LISTEN connection (`pglisten`, dialled through `store.Pool.Listener`) on `dispatch_events`, which migration `0107` notifies once per statement that inserts events. Every new connection signals too, to catch up on what it missed while it had none;
- the in-process events broker, so this process's own commits are seen while the LISTEN connection redials;
- every change of the NATS connection (`bus.Client.ConnectionChanged`), so a holder resends what a lost connection released;
- the holder's one retry timer, armed after each scan to the earliest due `next_attempt_at`, read as a duration on the database's clock. After a scan that failed it is armed to `retry.BaseDelay`.

The standby's is signalled only by the same LISTEN connection's `dispatch_duty_holds` notification of this hold's release, by every new LISTEN connection, and by every change of the NATS connection. The events the holder writes never wake a standby, so a standby sends no statement per event (`TestAStandbyDoesNotTryTheHoldForEveryEventTheHolderWrites`).

The holder scans on every wake. A standby only tries the hold: on its wake, on its timer to the current hold's lapse, or a second later when the database or its NATS probe did not answer. A scan runs up to 4 batches side by side (`concurrentBatches`): it starts one at once, another whenever the batch that returned published anything or was full, and another at every wake that reaches it while batches run - an insert of events, a reconnect, or an owner a batch gave back. A batch no longer blocks the next: a batch waiting out a role holder's receipt holds back its own owners alone, while another owner's batch is read, sent and recorded beside it. It starts no further batch after a batch that released an event or failed to read a row, after a statement that failed (the batch's read, a lookup other than a recipient read, or its record), and when the hold's deadline passed, a record found the hold another's, or NATS was lost; a batch that found nothing more to read ends nothing, since the batches still running give owners back. The scan returns only once every batch it started has returned, so `serve` answers a lost hold, a disconnection or the process's stop between scans. The batches of one scan run one statement at a time on the outbox's two connections (`lanes`), held around the statement and the drain of its rows and never across a send, so the hold's renewal never waits behind two of them.

## Owner lanes

Each owner is a lane one batch at a time runs in. A batch takes the owners of the rows it reads, and `readBatch` leaves out every owner the batches beside it hold (`coalesce(issue_key, artifact_id::text, project_key, '') <> all($2)`); the read and the taking happen under one lock, so two batches can never take one owner. So an owner's events keep their order across the batches running side by side, exactly as they keep it within one: an owner's later event is never read while an earlier one of it is still being sent.

A batch gives an owner's lane back as soon as the last chain of that owner has finished: it settles that owner's events, records them in one statement of their own (the hold check of every record, on the database's clock), releases the lane and wakes the scan, so the owner's next events and the owners the batch left behind are read while the rest of the batch is still sending. `deferPastWindow` runs per owner with that record. An owner with a send that failed for the connection waits for the batch's end instead, where the connection check below decides what its sends came to, and so does an owner with a send the hold's deadline fenced, one with an event released, and every owner of a batch whose context has ended. An owner whose own settle releases an event - a release the settle decides rather than one its sends foretold - is settled and recorded there, but reports the batch blocked as the batch's last settle does and keeps its lane to the batch's end all the same. A lane given back at the batch's end wakes nothing: the batch's own return is what the scan reads next, and a wake there would read again at once what a batch stopping for NATS has just released.

## The batch read

`readBatch` writes no row: with one publisher, no row needs a claim. In one statement it chooses up to 100 owners that have a due event: the lowest-id events never tried (`events_outbox_fresh`) and the earliest back-offs that have fallen due (`events_outbox_retry`). It then reads each chosen owner's unpublished events in id order, due or not, up to 100 per owner (`events_outbox_owner`, on `coalesce(issue_key, artifact_id::text, project_key)`). The batch keeps at most 100 due events. An owner is an issue, a document or a project. All three indexes are partial (migration `20261101143203_outbox_claim`) and exclude published rows and pending subscription commands. So a read never walks past backed-off rows of owners the batch does not choose, or past commands that are never published. An event with no owner (session-addressed, seq 0) has no destination and is marked published.

The followers, authors, recipients and mention deliveries a batch routes by are read once per batch: one set-based statement per kind (ask followers, thread root authors, message authors, document credits, the `comment_deliveries` of every new comment that names a mention at all and of every edit of one, which joins that same read, the sessions a person's reply reaches in its thread, each row marked as a member of the thread or as a credit of the thread's document, and the sessions of the primary document of an issue nobody routes or claims), and one more beside the mention read for the `comment_fallback_copies` rows that already own a destination of one (`loadMentionDeliveries`, two statements), so at most 8 lookup statements whatever the batch's size (`loadLookups`), and more only when a recipient read is run again in halves (below). On an issue a session claims, a person's comment obliges that session alone, and the credits of a document are not a second reason to deliver: a session the read gives only as a credit is left out (`addAuthorRoutes`), while a comment on a project document, an issue a person claims, an issue a route holds and no session claims, a member of the thread, a person's edit of a document (`artifact.human_edited`) and a session whose own mention of the comment failed, which gets its copy under the key of that failed mention, each keep the delivery they have now. An id is read in its canonical form, and one that is no uuid is never sent to Postgres: it fails the event that asked it, except a reply's `reply_to`, which then replies under no comment and so reaches no thread session, fails nothing and holds nothing back. A lookup statement that fails fails the batch, which is read again, except a recipient read (document credits, thread sessions, primary document sessions). A recipient statement Postgres refuses over the rows it read (`refusedStatement`) is run again in halves, and each refused half in halves, until each refused row is run alone (`loadRecipients`): an id in a run that answers gets its sessions, and only the events that asked an id whose own run is refused fail, whatever owners asked the statement's other ids. One broken row among n ids costs at most 2*ceil(log2(n)) more statements. A recipient statement that fails for the process's stop or for the connection (lost, refused at connect, or a server error of SQLSTATE class 08, 40, 53, 57 or 58, such as the `admin_shutdown` of a failover) is not run again and fails every event that asked it. A failed event's destinations end at the step that needed the read (`eventDestinations`): a new comment's mention read comes before the owner topic, so a new comment whose mention read failed has no destination at all, and every other read, an edit's mention read among them, comes after the owner topic, the route and the claimant, which stay in its list and are sent as any destination is, in the owner's order (below). Either way the event is backed off, or released when the process's stop failed the read. An event that is not due reads its recipients as a due one does, since they are what it holds its owner's later events back from. The issue's route and the session holding its claim (the claimant route, `addClaimantRoute`) are columns of the batch's read itself (`routing`), read again by every batch, so they cost no lookup.

A new comment's mention deliveries decide its session copies (`destinations.leftToMention`, asked where every copy bound for a session's own topic is added, `destinations.addSession`): a session a mention reached is skipped, one whose mention is still being sent (a `pending` attempt younger than `model.DeliveryClaimLapse`) has its copy held back, and every other is sent to as if it had not been mentioned. Which session a mention counts under is the attempt row's to say and never the payload's: an unanswered `@role:` mention names no session in the payload, its sender pinning one on the row after the commit, so a session only such a mention named is left to it as a named one is. A held-back copy is no destination of its event, so it holds none of the owner's later events back.

A copy of a mention reaches its session outside the chains: by the API's own send of the mention (`deliverResolvedCommentMention`, `internal/dispatch/api`), or by the fallback pass's publish of a `comment_fallback_copies` row (`decideFallbackCopy`). Each comes after every earlier event of the comment's owner that still owes a send that session may take (`model.MentionCopyHolderSQL`): a send to the owner's topic family, which the session may follow, one to the session's own key, or one an event whose recipient read failed may owe any session. A role route an earlier event owes is recorded as its role topic and holds the copy of the session that holds the role when the copy asks (`model.MentionCopyRolesSQL`): the API resolves the holder as it resolves a mention of the role, the fallback pass as a batch resolves a role route, and every answer but the listener's own that no live session holds the role (a 404 with reason `unclaimed` or `holder_lapsed`) or a holder that is another session holds the copy - a failure, a 500 for a claim it cannot read, an answer with no holder - so a holder that changed after the record is the one asked about, as `planChains` keys the route on whoever holds it at each batch. Both roads read the rule through one function, `copyrule.Holder` (`internal/dispatch/outbox/copyrule`), in one statement and so in one snapshot (`model.MentionCopyRuleSQL`): read as two, a route moved to the session and recorded between them would leave the first finding no key and the second no role, and the copy would pass the event; `TestOnlyHolderReadsTheCopyRule` fails on any other reader of the rule in the module, in `model.go` and `copyrule.go` too, where only the rule's own definitions and `Holder`'s body may name it. What an event still owes is what the batch last recorded on it (`events.owed_keys`, "The record" below), written again by every batch that reads it not due; an event no record has written that for owes its topic family at least, so it holds every copy until the outbox reads it - a new event has no record until its first batch, and a comment written straight after it would otherwise pass it. That record is trusted only while no event of the owner was written after the batch read it: `events.owed_seq` is the owner's `last_seq` the batch read beside the routing, and every write that makes a session a destination of the owner's events appends an event of the owner, which moves `last_seq` past it, so a route, a claim or a follower written since - before the comment or after it, and whatever record publishes that write's event - holds the copy until a batch that read the write records the event again. The rule compares `owed_seq` with the owner's newest event that is no pending subscription command: a command makes no session a destination and no batch reads it, so it leaves the record current. The exhaustive model of this order, `TestTheMentionCopyOrderModel` in `internal/dispatch/outbox`, runs every interleaving of such writes, two publishers whose hold moves (one of them the previous release in one run) and both roads of the copy, on the package's own `planChains`, `settleEvent` and `owedKeys`. The API's send that must wait makes no try: its attempt stays pending under its claim, and the mention sender moves it, with its owner's sends queued behind it, to a held lane of that owner and recipient that tries again 50 ms later, doubling to 2 s (`heldLane`, `mentionOrderRetry`); the recipient's sends of other owners go on, and each later try of a send that waited - behind an event, or behind none because its read of the rule failed - reads the rule alone, and makes the whole delivery only once the rule lets it go. Such a try writes no claim, so the sender keeps the send's claim among those it writes again, and a refresh that lands during the try records what it wrote; a try stays marked in flight until its park records its claim, so no re-read records the try's claim in between. A refresh whose answer is lost leaves the claim unknown, never assumed, and the sender re-reads the row before it acts on that send (`rereadUnsure`, `claimKnown`), applying a read only if no refresh lost its answer while it ran. The refresh writes a claim again only while the claim it names is live (`refreshMentionClaims`), and the re-read and the lane's own send take a row's claim as the sender's own while it is at or past the claim recorded and less than a lease after it (`model.DeliveryClaimSinceSQL`), a window no other sender's claim falls in. After a lease with no committed refresh - a database the sender cannot reach - a claim lapses, as a stopped process's claims do, and the delivery sweep may take the attempt. A try that ends not waiting while a hand-off - the delivery sweep's or a keyed repeat's - moved the recorded claim is tried again under that claim, and a send that ends releases its attempt under the lock that found no hand-off, so one that lands after it queues the attempt anew (`claimedDuring`). A send parked because its claim could not be read waits behind no event, so it is no held copy and logs no WARN, and so does a send whose read of the copy rule failed: it keeps its claim and its place on its lane, so its owner's later comments to the session stay behind it. Each of its retries reads the rule alone, for the session it waits to reach, and while the read keeps failing it logs an ERROR and parks again with no claim and no write, so each such lane head reads the rule and logs once each 2 s or less. After a claim lapses the delivery sweep sends owed attempts side by side and keeps no order between two comments, as after a crash. The sender's part of the model, `TestTheMentionSenderClaimModel` in `internal/dispatch/api`, drives the real sender in a `testing/synctest` bubble through its lock (`mentionSender.mu`, a `sync.Locker` that is a plain `*sync.Mutex` in every process and the model's own lock there), so each of its locked sections and each of its statements is a step; it runs every interleaving of them with the rows' claims, refresh answers lost after their statement commits, re-reads that fail and, in its full run, refreshes that fail before they commit, the delivery sweep and the earlier events' sends, and checks that no claim the sender trusts falls behind its row, that no attempt pending under a live claim is left with no sender holding it, that every held send's wait names a real earlier event, and that two comments to one session arrive in order, counting apart an A-2 the sweep sends first after its own claim lapsed. The model names each function that takes the sender's lock by the name of its frame (`mentionSenderLockSites`), and `TestTheLockSiteListNamesTheSendersLocks` type-checks the package in every run of it and holds that list equal to the functions that call `Lock` on the sender's mutex, through any value of the sender's type or a local variable that holds the mutex; it fails as well on a lock whose frame names no function - in a function literal, in a range over a function, deferred or started on a goroutine - and on the mutex handed anywhere it cannot follow. A row that must wait is read again `mentionPendingDelay` (2 s) later. So N held lanes, behind an event or a failed read, and M held rows, once their waits reach 2 s, read the rule at most N + M times each 2 s, and neither wait holds anything of the owner's later events back. The wait has no bound, so it shows: gauges count the held copies and read the oldest one's age, with no label, since `/metrics` answers whoever reaches the port and an owner's key is neither bounded nor public (`envoy_dispatch_mention_sends_held`, `envoy_dispatch_outbox_fallback_copies_held` and their `_oldest_seconds`), and a copy held a minute (`model.MentionCopyHeldWarnAfter`) logs one WARN naming the owner, the earlier event and its `publish_error`, beside the ERROR the outbox logs at each failed publish of that event. In a deployment with no outbox (`DISPATCH_NATS_DISABLED=1`) nothing is ever published, and the API's send waits for nothing (`api.Deps.OutboxPublishes`). The order between two comments' mention copies to one session is the mention sender's own, one lane per recipient in one process, except that a held lane's comments come after the other owners' comments that went past them.

A `comment_fallback_copies` row of a comment its author has deleted is withdrawn. The delete marks the comment deleted, fails its pending mention attempts and settles the rows it finds with nothing published, in one commit (`deleteComment`, `internal/dispatch/api`). A failed mention is the state in which the fallback pass publishes a row, so the pass reads whether the comment is deleted after every other read of its decision (`fallbackCopyWithdrawn`) and settles a row of a deleted comment with nothing sent: one it claimed before the delete and decided after it, and one a batch that read the mention before the delete held after it, which the delete never saw. Only a copy the pass had decided to publish before the delete committed still goes, a send in flight. The event's own `comment.created`, held back while a mention is being sent, is no such row: it goes out as recorded, and the delete's `comment.deleted` follows it.

## Order

Order is kept per (owner, destination key), and an owner's topic family comes before the owner's later sends to every other key (below). A destination key is the subject family a session receives on: the owner's topic family under `notifications.dispatch.` is one, and each `notifications.agent.<session>` subject is one, whichever route reaches it. A role route's key is its holder's agent subject, and its role topic while no holder is known - a copy kept for the role's next holder, and a claim no read could resolve, among those (`destination.key`). `planChains` builds one chain per owner and key, in id order. `sendChains` sends every chain through one window, and sends a chain's next envelope only once the answer to the one before it came back as reached or refused. So no two sends of one (owner, destination key) are ever in flight, and different owners and keys proceed side by side, save the wait on the topic family below.

A destination that stalls (a send that failed, or an event waiting out a back-off with that destination unreached) holds back that destination's later events of that owner and nothing else, unless it is the owner's topic family; the two wider holds are the topic family's and that of an event whose recipient read failed, both below. Such a held-back event is recorded `blocked`: it falls due when the earliest of its blockers does, with no attempt counted, and is read again beside its blocker. A blocker of the same batch that was released releases the event it held back too. An event with an owner and no destination - one that cannot be read at all (a scan or decode failure), one whose envelope cannot be built or validated (`resolveDestinations`), and a new comment whose mention deliveries could not be read (`eventDestinations`) - is backed off and holds nothing back.

The owner's topic family holds back every key. A session that follows an owner's topic family and is also sent the owner's events on its own subject receives on both, and its relay hands it both in stream order. The outbox cannot know which sessions follow the owner, so an earlier event's topic send that has not gone, and that NATS has not refused for good, comes before each later send of the owner to any key. An earlier event that is not due, with the topic family neither reached (`published_destinations`) nor refused for good (`refused_destinations`), holds the owner's later events back from every key, behind its `next_attempt_at`. In one batch, whose chains start at once, a send to any other key waits for the owner's last topic send of an earlier event in that batch (`chainSend.after`): `sendChains` makes it once that send reached the topic family or was refused for good, and when it failed or was held back, holds it back with the rest of its chain, behind the event whose failed send stopped the topic chain. A topic send refused for good is never sent again, so no later send can overtake it. The hold is one-sided: a session key reaches its one session, which takes the owner's events in order from the topic family as well, so a session key that stalls holds back that key alone and the topic family goes on. The cost: an owner's sends to session keys wait for that owner's earlier topic sends to be answered. In one batch that is one publish round trip for each earlier topic send of the owner still queued in the batch, since the topic chain sends one at a time and the send waits for the last of them; while the topic family cannot be published, it is the whole back-off.

A mention's copies are sent outside `planChains`: the API's own mention send is no outbox send, and a `comment_fallback_copies` row is published by the fallback pass. Each still comes after every earlier event of its owner that owes a send its session may take (`model.MentionCopyHolderSQL`, described above), but holds none of the owner's later events back: a row exists to bound a wait (`unnamedForwardBound`, a past-lease copy), and holding the owner's later events behind it would bring that wait back.

An event whose recipient read failed has a list that ends at that read, so it may still be bound for any session the read would name. Every later event of its owner is held back from each session key (an agent subject, or a role route's key) that the failed event neither names in its list nor already reached (`published_destinations`) or had refused (`refused_destinations`), behind a blocker that falls due when it is tried again: its back-off or release when it was due, its `next_attempt_at` when it was not. A key its list names chains as usual, a key it already reached or had refused holds nothing back, and the owner's topic family is never held this way. So a row no recipient read can take, such as a credit list `jsonb_array_elements` refuses, backs off the events that read it and holds back their owners' later session copies on the keys those events could still owe, until their retry. The two holds add up: a later event is held back from a session key by either. An event whose reads all answer, due or not, holds back only the destinations it has neither reached nor had refused, and every key while one of them is the owner's topic family.

## Role routes

A `role:<id>` route is not published to `notifications.role.<id>`: nothing of Dispatch's crosses `notifications.role.*` any more. The outbox resolves the role's holder itself with `roles.ResolveHolder`, the one resolution the listener's role routes and its role lane also use, so the three cannot drift. It resolves once per role per batch, reading the role and session buckets directly (`roles.Buckets` on the outbox's bus client) and writing to neither: no outcome here takes a claim from a holder. The outcomes:

- no claim: the copy is kept on the role's kept lane for one hour for the role's next live holder (`keptOrSkipped`), and the destination counts as reached once the stream acknowledges it;
- a claim whose holder's session is gone - its own last registration past the gone horizon, or nothing the lookup remembers dates it any later: the copy is kept as it is for an unclaimed role, and the claim is left where it is for the listener's role-claim reaper, the one caller that ever releases one;
- a claim whose holder is unresponsive - past the live window and inside the gone horizon: pending, backed off with an attempt counted and retried, so the event waits for that session to answer rather than being published with nothing sent. Nothing is kept for it. The outbox sends the event again until the session answers and the forward reaches it, or until the session's record passes the gone horizon and the copy is kept as it is for a gone holder. A copy kept for the unresponsive holder would wait one hour at most, and the end of that hour would tell nobody, since an outbox copy gets no dead letter. The listener's own publish route and role lane do keep a copy for an unresponsive holder, so this is the one resolution the outbox and the listener keep differently;
- a holder nothing dates at all - no record in the session bucket and no memory of one - pending too, since a bucket's maximum age can be shorter than the gone horizon;
- a read that failed: pending, backed off with an attempt counted and retried;
- the holder is the event's own actor session (the listener's echo skip): reached, nothing sent;
- otherwise a forward to `notifications.agent.<holder>` with a window `Request`. It carries the envelope a listener forward carries: topic `notifications.role.<id>`, dedupe key `envoy.role.forward.` plus the event's key (`contracts.RoleForwardDedupePrefix`), event id unchanged. It is sent under a `Nats-Msg-Id` of that key and subject (`bus.ForwardMsgID`), so the stream stores one copy however often it is sent. The listener's own role forward (`roleTopicDelivery`, `cmd/listener/delivery.go`) is the same stored-only send (`bus.Client.StartRequestStoredTo`, `startRoleForward`), and passes that MsgId only when the original envelope's key names its event (`contracts.DedupeKeyNamesTheUpstreamEvent`, asked of the envelope before the prefix is added), which every Dispatch envelope's does, and the envelope's event id and the subject otherwise (`roleForward.msgID`, `cmd/listener/role_lane.go`).

The stream's acknowledgement that it stored the forward for the holder's own consumer decides it, within `roleForwardAckTimeout` (5 s, `forward`, `internal/dispatch/outbox/role.go`), and no sender waits for a receipt: the holder is delivered that stored copy by the listener's relay and answers the forward with nothing, whatever its entry holds. Acknowledged, the destination is reached, for every holder alike. An acknowledgement that does not arrive within that window, and one the stream answers with an error, each leave the destination pending and retried, which that `Nats-Msg-Id` makes idempotent at the stream. The host plugins apply their dedupe to the forward unchanged.

A role token no claim can ever name (`roles.Valid`, the pattern `POST /v1/roles/set` refuses) is kept for nobody: the destination is reached with nothing sent, as every unheld role was before the kept lane, since a copy kept under such a token waits for a claim that cannot be made. The listener's own keep refuses the same token (`keepForNextHolder`), which the two writers of a role's kept lane must agree on.

The keep is one JetStream publish of the envelope as its sender sent it, on the role's kept subject (`notifications.envoy.kept.role.<id>`, `bus.KeptRoleSubject`) and under the id every writer of that lane keeps the message under (`bus.KeptMsgID` of the envelope's own dedupe key and the role), so a copy the listener kept and the outbox's own are one stored message. Acknowledged, the destination is reached; anything else leaves the event pending for the outbox's own retry, which that MsgId makes idempotent at the stream.

That acknowledgement also starts a handover: the batch reads the role's claim again, and when it names a live holder and the copy just kept is the first on that role's lane past its drain cursor with no walk's claim outstanding, it forwards the event to that holder before it answers the keep (`handOverKept`). The claim is read before the lane because a copy is kept only for a role no live holder can take, so the resolution that still finds none ends the handover on that one read. This closes the race the batch's two moments leave open: it resolves the role as it opens and keeps at its end, so a claim that lands between them starts a drain of a lane the copy is not on yet (`cmd/listener/role_keep.go`, `drainKeptRole`) and nothing walks that lane again until the listener's sweep, while the issue's next event resolves the holder live and is forwarded straight to its agent subject - the holder would take event N + 1 minutes before event N. The forward goes under the id the drain's own forward of that copy would carry, so the stream's duplicate window and the holder's dedupe key collapse whichever of the two arrives second; it carries the time of the keep as `kept_at`, which the drain's forward carries as the stream's store time of the copy, since every holder is reached through the stream, which keeps whichever copy arrives first - this one, unless a drain that read the cursor before the handover reaches the agent subject ahead of it; it is made before the keep's answer, so the kept copy reaches that holder before any send the batches after this one make for the owner; and the copy stays on the lane, whose cursor and drain stay the authority for a holder that claims later.

The lane read is what keeps the handover behind the copies already on that lane: a drain hands a lane over strictly in order from its cursor (`walkKeptRole`), so forwarding the copy just kept while older ones wait there would overtake them, and a role token names one tree role of one issue, so those older copies are that issue's own - its holder would take a later event before an earlier one. A copy that is not the first past the cursor is therefore left to the claimant's own drain, which finds those copies and this one behind them and sends the lot in order, without waiting for the sweep either; a lane the copy is alone on is the race above, and the handover fires there exactly as it did before the read. One copy on that lane is not in the handover's way: the one the same batch handed to that same holder, since a handover writes no cursor and leaves its copy where it is, so the batch records per role what its own handovers delivered and to which holder (`keptHandovers`, made in `sendChains` and dropped with the batch) and reads the lane from past both that and the cursor - without it, two events of one issue kept in one batch would strand the second behind a copy the holder already has, until the sweep. The holder is part of that record because it stands for "this holder already has everything up to here": a role whose claim changed hands between two of the batch's keeps resolves a holder that was handed none of those copies, and for it the cursor alone says what leads the lane. The reads are two stored-message reads of the stream (`bus.Client.ReadKeptRoleCursor` of the cursor, `NextStoredMessage` of the first copy past it), made through `Deps.KeptLanes`, the bus client `cmd/dispatch` passes, which a `Deps` without one leaves making no handover at all; the cursor read is the listener's own pass's as well (`readKeptLaneHead`), and the grant is one the outbox already holds. The cursor's body and the rule for whether a walk's claim is outstanding are defined once, in the package both writers of a lane import (`bus.KeptRoleCursor`, `ClaimOutstanding`, `bus.KeptRoleClaimWindow`), because a body or a window written twice is how the two drift apart; a claim a killed drain left behind declines the handover until it is past that window, and no longer, since the next walk takes a claim that old over and the sweep guarantees a walk. The cursor, the claim and the ordering stay the listener's to own, and the outbox grows no second drain of them.

A read or a forward of the handover that failed is logged at Warn and nothing more, a read the process's own stop cancelled excepted: the destination was reached when the keep was acknowledged, so the failure is not counted among the destinations a publish did not reach (`countPublishFailure`), which are the ones the outbox sends again, and nothing retries the handover. What it costs is that issue's order at the holder: the copy reaches it at the role's next claim, keep or the listener's sweep, up to five minutes later, behind every event the holder takes meanwhile.

An outbox copy waits on the role's kept lane for one hour from the time the stream stored it, as the listener's own copies do. The listener's walk hands it to the role's next live holder inside that hour, and drops it at the end of the hour, whether or not any session holds the role (`judgeKeptRoleCopy`, `cmd/listener/role_keep.go`), counted under the label `kept_by` `dispatch_outbox`. The listener knows an outbox copy by its source `dispatch` and a dedupe key that does not begin with `publish.` (`isOutboxCopy`). Nobody is sent a dead letter for an outbox copy, so the person or session that wrote the Dispatch event is not told that no holder took it. An outbox copy raises no wake report either.

Residual: a keep's key is its role topic, since the batch knew no holder when it planned its chains, so an event of the same owner that the same batch sends to that session on a key of its own - an author or follower route among them - races the handover, as two keys of one owner always do (Order, above). Before the handover that copy waited for the role's next claim or the sweep and lost to every such event.

## Failure classes

Once every answer of a batch is in:

- **released**, with no attempt counted and due again at once: a send that met a lost or replaced connection (`bus.ErrUnavailable`) or the process stopping. Released events are sent again on the reconnect's wake. When the batch's connection was replaced since it began, or its probe failed (NATS lost, above), the not-acknowledged sends are released too. A batch in which a send met the hold's deadline is left unrecorded instead, not released (Fencing, above);
- **backed off**, with the attempt counted and the error recorded in `events.publish_error`: a send NATS denied (`bus.ErrPublishDenied`) or did not acknowledge on a connection still current (`bus.ErrNotAcknowledged`: no acknowledgement in time, no responders, no receipt from a role's holder), a topic the stream does not capture (`bus.ErrNotCaptured`), a role route left pending, or an event whose destinations could not all be resolved. The delay is `retry.Delay` of the attempt count, capped at 5 min, added to the database's clock (`now() + make_interval(...)`);
- **blocked**, with no attempt counted: besides an event an earlier event holds back (Order, above), an event that held a session's copy back for a mention still being sent. Such an event falls due `mentionPendingDelay` (2 s) later at the latest, when its mention deliveries are read again, or sooner when an earlier event holding it back falls due sooner. A held-back copy blocks the event only when no send of it failed or was lost: an event that also failed or met a lost connection is backed off or released as it would be without the mention;
- **refused**, finished with no retry and no attempt counted: a send NATS refuses the same way however often it is sent (`bus.ErrRefused`: past the max payload, or a subject NATS would not take). It is counted and logged (`dispatch outbox: destination refused`) and sent once more with the payload left out (`standInEnvelope`). A payload that is not a JSON object becomes `{"payload_omitted":true}`, so every event has a stand-in. A stand-in NATS takes reaches the destination. A stand-in NATS refuses too is logged (`dispatch outbox: the stand-in was refused too`) and not counted again, and the event goes on to its other destinations. The record keeps that subject and its error in `events.refused_destinations` and out of `published_destinations`, and no retry sends it again. A batch that stops before the stand-in goes releases the event, and the next scan sends both, so it counts and logs the refusal again. A full stream (`bus.ErrStreamFull`) and a topic the stream does not capture are no refusal: they back off.

## The record

`recordBatch` writes what one settlement came to in one statement, so all of it or none, and an event row is written once per attempt: one statement per owner a batch settles before it ends ("Owner lanes" above), and one for everything it settles at its end. It appends each reached destination to `published_destinations`, and merges each refused one, with its error, into `refused_destinations`. It sets `published_at` and clears `publish_error` once every destination is reached or refused. Behind the hold check of "Fencing" above, it writes back-off (`next_attempt_at`, `attempt_count`, `publish_error`), hold-back (`next_attempt_at` alone: the earlier of its delay out, which a blocker's back-off or a mention still being sent gives it, and a not-due blocker's due time), release (`next_attempt_at = null`), and what the event still owes (`owed_keys`, `owedKeys`): the key of each destination of its list no batch has reached or had refused, a waiting copy's included and a row's copy and a role route reached with nothing to send left out, the owner's topic family spelled `topic-family`, a role route spelled as its role topic whatever holder it resolved to, and `any-session` beside them when its recipient read failed. Beside them it writes `owed_seq`, the owner's `last_seq` the batch's read saw (`pendingEvent.ownerSeq`), and never one without the other. An event the batch read not due, only as a blocker, has its `owed_keys` and `owed_seq` written alone, and only when either changed (`owedAgain`). Every row is guarded on `published_at is null`. A record that fails leaves what it was to write as it was read, as a process that dies between NATS's acknowledgements and the record does. The next scan or the next holder sends it again: JetStream drops a repeat by MsgId inside its 72-hour duplicate window, and a role's holder drops a repeat forward by its dedupe key. Delivery is therefore at least once, and each receiver takes each event once.
